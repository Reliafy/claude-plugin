# Analysing RBDs in Reliafy

- rbd, non-repairable: reliability curve (~21 points; `times` gives exact values in reliability_at), MTTF, B-lives, importance and cut sets; current_state ({node: {failed: true} | {age}}) gives the remaining life from now; target_reliability the design life; confidence intervals from fitted blocks (with b_lives, times).
- rbd, repairable, on every plan: exact (or numerical, never simulated) long-run availability, mean up / down time, failure frequency, and in `exact` A(t), the mission availability and the window's expected failures, outages, downtime and cost — from new, or from now with current_state ({node: {down: true, since} | {age}}) over t_max. Safety functions add PFDavg and SIL; priced diagrams costs (a present value with discount_rate); capacities the production availability. The Monte-Carlo simulation adds only the spread (percentiles, P(no outage), criticality; from current_state the time to the next failure) and is Pro; it runs only where a figure needs it unless simulate=true. A saved result is served when one matches. When a response says figures are simulation-only (e.g. one crew for wear-out lives), say so; offer rbd_export_python to run it locally. summary_only gives the headline figures.
- Jobs: a long simulation or calculation returns a job_id: call get kind job every few seconds until done. rbd_quote_simulation says how long one should take before anything runs; list kind rbd_runs / get kind rbd_run read past simulations.
- What to improve (repairable): rbd_sensitivity ranks every lever by what a 10% step gains (exact or numerical free; simulated Pro); costs_to_change with order benefit_per_cost ranks by benefit per cost; measure gives over_time, shares, joint, rate or uncertainty. Quote `top` with its basis.
- rbd_optimise_intervals chooses age-replacement or proof-test intervals together (lowest cost, or a target availability / PFDavg / SIL, or a cost cap), returns edit_rbd_ops to apply with change rbd_edit. rbd_cheapest_design: the redundancy with the lowest total cost (needs block acquisition prices, a horizon and a downtime_rate; trains copy whole chains). rbd_compare: design B against A (change rbd_clone A, edit, compare). rbd_allocate_targets: what each block needs for a system target. rbd_fault_tree: ranked cut sets with common causes. These answer only; apply a choice with change rbd_edit.
- rbd_system_history: observed availability from the diagram's outage log. rbd_export_python: a standalone script (SurPyval + RePyability) with the run command; rbd_export_json: RePyability's JSON (change import_rbd takes it back).

## The actions

Every call is `tool(action=…, args={…})` (`kind=…` for get and list), the action's arguments in `args`. `?` marks an optional argument; `guide(action='<tool>.<action>')` returns an action's full argument schema.

- `analyse(action='rbd', args={rbd_id, times?, t_max?, conditional_age?, recompute?, simulate?, current_state?, compute_exact?, target_reliability?, confidence?, b_lives?, include_curves?, discount_rate?, summary_only?})`: Analyse a saved diagram.
- `analyse(action='rbd_quote_simulation', args={rbd_id, t_max?, current_state?})`: How long a repairable diagram's availability simulation should take, before anything runs (estimate, median_s, p90_s), and how many jobs are queued ahead.
- `analyse(action='rbd_sensitivity', args={rbd_id, window?, step?, rank_by?=availability, costs_to_change?, order?=benefit, simulate?=true, limit?=15, lever_limits?, measure?=levers, group_by?=block, uncertainty_method?=delta, times?})`: What to improve first in a repairable diagram: every lever (each block's mean life, mean repair time and intervals, its coverage, one more crew) moved a step (default 10%) the way that helps, ranked by the gain in availability (or cost), in plain words; with costs_to_change and order='benefit_per_cost', by benefit per cost.
- `analyse(action='rbd_optimise_intervals', args={rbd_id, schedule?, blocks?, min_availability?, max_pfd?, target_sil?, max_cost_rate?, allowed_intervals?, stagger_tests?, assume_unlimited_crews?, simulate_with_crews?})`: Choose a repairable diagram's age-replacement or proof-test intervals together: the lowest cost, the cheapest meeting min_availability (safety function: max_pfd or target_sil), or the most available within max_cost_rate.
- `analyse(action='rbd_cheapest_design', args={rbd_id, horizon?, min_availability?, blocks?, discount_rate?, trains?, horizons?})`: The redundancy that owns a repairable diagram at the lowest total cost: active copies of priced blocks (and of whole trains) weighed against the downtime they save, optionally keeping min_availability; the design as drawn and the cheapest side by side, the copies and the saving, over several horizons (by_horizon).
- `analyse(action='rbd_compare', args={rbd_id, other_rbd_id, t_max?})`: Is design B (other_rbd_id) better than A?
- `analyse(action='rbd_fault_tree', args={rbd_id, t?, max_cut_sets?=20, max_events?=15, include_gates?})`: The diagram as a fault tree: the top event probability (F(t) at t, or a repairable diagram's long-run unavailability), minimal cut sets ranked by probability with share_alone (they overlap: the shares don't add up to 1), basic events by criticality with Birnbaum, Fussell-Vesely, RAW and RRW, and each common-cause group's shared cause.
- `analyse(action='rbd_allocate_targets', args={rbd_id, target, method?, t?, availability_method?, levers?, fixed?, weights?, maximum?, feasibility?, max_mttf?, min_mttr?, mttf_feasibility?, mttr_feasibility?})`: What each block needs for the system to meet a target (a reliability at t, or a repairable diagram's availability with the MTTF / MTTR that give it), against what it has now, by method.
- `analyse(action='rbd_system_history', args={rbd_id, log_id?})`: A diagram's observed history from its outage log (the latest unless log_id): the availability, outages, failures vs planned, downtime and observed MTBF over the window, the longest system outages with the block that caused each, and the blocks ranked by share of downtime.
- `analyse(action='rbd_export_python', args={rbd_id})`: A standalone Python script (SurPyval + RePyability) that rebuilds and analyses the diagram offline, with the command to run it: also how to run its simulation for free.
- `analyse(action='rbd_export_json', args={rbd_id})`: The diagram in RePyability's JSON (rbd_from_json loads it; change import_rbd takes it back without loss), with notes on what only the file's reliafy part holds.
- `get(kind='job', args={id})`: A queued calculation (an availability simulation, a what-to-improve or interval search, an ALT's bootstrap bounds): status queued (queue_position), running, done (the result, as the action that queued it returns it) or failed (message).
- `get(kind='rbd_run', args={id, include_curve?})`: One availability simulation in full: its results with intervals, the inputs it ran on (and whether the diagram changed since), engine versions, the precision reached and what was quoted before it ran.
- `list(kind='rbd_runs', args={rbd_id?, limit?=20})`: Availability simulations, newest first, from the app or any connector: who ran each and from where, status, method, runtime, replications, window and the simulated availability with its interval.
